###### 5200 Final Project 
<sub> Introduction and Literature Review: 
This study employed a mixed-methods quantitative approach combining publicly available aggregate time series data from multiple institutional sources to examine associations between male social disconnection indicators and domestic violence homicide in the United States from 1996 to 2023. The analysis was structured around a theoretically derived causal chain drawing on peer-reviewed literature in criminology, public health, and the sociology of masculinity.<sub> 

<sub> Data Collection: 
Data were drawn from six primary sources. Intimate partner femicide and total female homicide counts were sourced from the Violence Policy Center's annual "When Men Murder Women" reports, which represent the most consistent long-run series available for this metric in the United States. Male intimate partner homicide, family-context DV homicide, and collateral victim estimates were derived from the Bureau of Justice Statistics Homicide Trends reports and extended using proportional estimates from CDC WISQARS data. Murder-suicide counts in DV contexts were drawn from the Violence Policy Center's "American Roulette" series. Annual unemployment rates were obtained from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The percentage of men living alone was drawn from US Census Bureau America's Families and Living Arrangements tables, with annual values interpolated between survey years using index-based interpolation. The percentage of men reporting no close friends was sourced from the Survey Center on American Life at the American Enterprise Institute, with seven survey data points spanning 1990 to 2021 interpolated to annual values. International comparison data on intimate partner homicide rates and IPV prevalence were retrieved from Our World in Data's grapher API, drawing on underlying data from the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime and the World Bank respectively.<sub> 

<sub> All data were merged on calendar year and analyzed using Python 3 with the pandas, NumPy, SciPy, matplotlib, seaborn, Plotly, and Bokeh libraries. Pearson correlation coefficients were computed for all pairings of isolation indicators and DV homicide outcome variables, with 95 percent confidence intervals derived via Fisher z-transformation and two-tailed p-values reported. Spearman rank correlations were computed in parallel to assess robustness to distributional assumptions. Variance inflation factors were computed to assess multicollinearity between predictor variables. The Great Recession period (2008 to 2010) was examined as a natural experiment by comparing mean DV homicide counts across six economic periods: pre-recession (2005 to 2007), recession (2008 to 2010), recovery (2011 to 2013), low unemployment (2017 to 2019), COVID Era (2020 to 2021), and Post-COVID Era (2022 to 2023). <sub> 

<sub> Methods: 
Several methodological limitations constrain interpretation. The male friendship series is interpolated from only seven survey data points and should be treated as indicative rather than precise. All series share secular time trends over the study period, which inflates Pearson correlations between any two upward-trending variables regardless of causal relationship; detrending or first-differencing was not applied to the primary analysis and represents a necessary next step before regression modeling. The analysis operates entirely at the aggregate level, making individual-level causal inference impossible. Key theoretical mediators including masculinity threat perception and coercive control behavior are not measured in any of the datasets used. Economic precarity is represented only by the official unemployment rate, leaving wage stagnation, housing cost burden, underemployment, and wealth inequality unmeasured. Femicide counts from the Violence Policy Center exclude multi-offender and multi-victim incidents and may undercount true femicide by 15 to 30 percent relative to CDC NVDRS estimates.<sub> 

<sub> Results: 
Five visualization outputs were produced: two static multi-panel figures using matplotlib and seaborn, two interactive figures using Plotly with hover tooltips and dropdown controls, and one linked-view dashboard using Bokeh which a shared ColumnDataSource and RangeTool enabled synchronized pan and zoom across all panels. A supplementary causal inference panel was produced to illustrate the conceptual distinctions between correlation, confounding, mediation, collision bias, multicollinearity, and spurious association, with each concept grounded in examples drawn directly from the study variables. The Infographic, Map-Based and Temporal visualization were supplemented from resources such as the Office of the U.S. Surgeon General and Our World in Data. <sub>
